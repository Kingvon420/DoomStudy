What AI changes

You're right that AI moved fast. A solo founder with AI can now build what used to need a 20-person studio for the first year. One thing hasn't moved, though, and the whole plan is designed around it.

What AI makes possible now
  • Writing the engine, netcode and game rules (me)
  • Prototyping a new game mode in a day instead of months
  • Generating 3D models, textures and animations from text or video
  • Smart teammates and opponents that play like people
  • One person running what used to need a whole studio
What it can't do
  • Take the actual Madden, 2K or COD and merge them. Their code and assets belong to EA, Take-Two and Microsoft
  • Ship ripped game files without getting taken down and sued
  • Make a jailbroken console into a business. Sony patches it, and selling it is illegal

Steve showing up in GTA 5 is a mod: a fan's personal copy, tolerated because nobody sells it. That's a great way to learn, but it can't be the product. The play is to build your own games in those genres, inside your own world. The brands come later, once you have the players.

The modder's road

Gaming history backs this plan. The biggest genres started as mods of someone else's game, then broke out as original games:

Half-Life1998→Counter-Strike mod1999→Counter-Strikeits own franchise
Warcraft III2002→DotA custom map2003→League & Dota 2the MOBA genre
Arma 2 / 3→Battle royale modsearly 2010s→PUBG → Fortnite BR2017

The pattern: prove the idea fast, then rebuild it as something you own. Switchboard follows the same path.

The build, phase by phase

✓

Prove the concept

DONE

The browser prototype: a plaza, a crew that follows you, three plug-in games and one shared wallet.

Proves
The kernel + cartridge design works. A new game is about 150 lines.
1

Real friends, real party

WEEKS 1–4

DRE, MIA and KAI become four real people. Make a party code, text it to your friends, and everyone's in the plaza together.

Build
Game server (Node.js + WebSockets), party codes, everyone's moves synced, voice chat later
Milestone
4 phones in one Hoops game, scores counting for the whole team
You learn
Client vs. server, and why the server is the referee (anti-cheat starts here)
Cost
Free to about $20/month in hosting (estimate)
2

Go 3D

MONTHS 1–3

Rebuild the plaza and the games in 3D while staying in the browser, so joining is still just tapping a link. No download means faster growth.

Build
Three.js world, a third-person camera, AI-generated 3D characters and courts
Milestone
Walk through a 3D city into a 3D court and shoot on a real hoop
You learn
3D coordinates, cameras, animation, and how an engine draws a frame
Later move
Unreal or Godot for console-level graphics. The cartridge rules carry over unchanged.
3

Three games people replay

MONTHS 3–9

This phase decides whether Switchboard makes it. Every game has to be fun enough to keep people coming back. That takes depth, not more games.

Build
Real 2v2/3v3 basketball, 7-on-7 football, squad shooter. Ranked modes. One account, one avatar, one inventory across all three.
Milestone
100 players who come back every week without being asked
You learn
Game feel, balancing, and reading player data
Help needed
A contract 3D artist/animator, plus friends playtesting every week
4

Open the doors to creators

YEAR 1–2

This is the Linux moment. Publish the cartridge rules so anyone can build a game into the world: kart racing, golf, fighting, horror. Each one gets a building in the city.

Build
A creator kit, sandboxing so a bad game can't crash the world, a revenue share for creators
Milestone
A game you didn't make becomes the most-played building in the city
Model
How Roblox and Fortnite Creative grew from a handful of modes to millions of games
5

The brands come to you

ONCE YOU HAVE PLAYERS

With a real audience, licensing becomes a business deal instead of a lawsuit. Leagues and publishers chase players. Fortnite already sells NFL and NBA gear, and Roblox has league-licensed games.

Build
Real teams and athletes (league + players' association licenses), publisher partnerships, an official building for a big franchise
Milestone
A major sports or shooter brand opens an official venue in your city
